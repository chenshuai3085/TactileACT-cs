\section{Data Collection and Format}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Robot Observation Schema}

All policies and foresight modules are trained from timestamp-aligned HDF5
episodes. A trajectory contains synchronized RGB views, proprioception, action
labels, tactile marker fields, and optional force/torque measurements. The main
action-conditioned foresight contract is
\begin{equation}
\hat{\latent}_{t:t+H-1}
= f_\psi(I_t^g, I_t^w, \marker_{t-W+1:t}, q_t,
\action_{t:t+H-1}),
\end{equation}
where the condition is the future action chunk
\texttt{actions/joint\_abs[t:t+H]}, not the future state trajectory. This
distinction is important: future states are outcomes, while the rollout-time
quantity available before execution is the candidate action chunk.

RGB images are resized and cropped before ImageNet normalization. The tactile
input is a GelSight marker displacement field, normalized per displacement
channel. TacVAE consumes an eight-frame tactile window and encodes the current
contact into a compact spatial latent. The diffusion policy uses
\texttt{obs\_horizon}=2 and predicts a 16-step action chunk for the main
experiments.

\subsection{Collection Procedure}

Each demonstration episode is recorded as a continuous robot execution trace.
The controller saves either joint-absolute commands
(\path{actions/joint_abs}) or end-effector commands
(\path{actions/eef_abs} / \path{eef_rel}), depending on the task script. The
final action-conditioned \method{} experiments use joint-absolute chunks for
board, card, and socket-style policies when those labels match the measured
joint states. Vase data collected with older end-effector scripts is handled
separately because some saved \path{joint_abs} labels contain inverse-kinematics
branch jumps that are not present in the measured robot motion. For those
sequences, end-effector labels or regenerated joint labels should be used for
policy training.

The tactile stream is saved at the same episode step index as the RGB and robot
state streams. The most important tactile field for this paper is
\texttt{marker\_offset}, a $9\times9$ grid of two-dimensional marker
displacements. We prefer this marker field over raw tactile RGB for the
foresee-and-score pipeline because it is compact, directly related to local
contact deformation, and can be visualized as a deformed grid. Force/torque
signals are used for labeling and analysis when available, but the main
foresee-and-score chain does not require force sensing at deployment.

\subsection{TacVAE Pretraining Corpus}

TacVAE is pretrained on the full tactile corpus rather than only on the
five-task rollout split. The pretraining corpus contains 5,969 HDF5
episodes and approximately 2.1 million tactile marker frames. The corpus
includes successful contact,
bounce, too-small-force, too-large-force, oscillatory-force, insertion
misalignment, and replayed trajectory variants. These negative or imperfect
segments are useful for representation learning because the encoder must
preserve both desirable and undesirable contact states; the contact-quality
scorer later learns which regions of this tactile space should be preferred.

Task-local TacVAE checkpoints use the same architecture and normalization
contract for board wiping, vase wiping, and card swiping. These task-local
encoders are used when the deployment policy is task-specific; the full-corpus
TacVAE is used as a broader tactile pretraining reference.

\subsection{Task Labels and Safety Definitions}

For each online trial, a success is counted when the task objective is completed
within the rollout horizon. A safe success is stricter and is computed from the
saved rollout log. Let $y_{\rm task}\in\{0,1\}$ denote task completion, $F_t$ the
contact-force magnitude, $\mathcal{C}=\{t:F_t>\tau_{\rm contact}\}$ the contact
frames, $r$ the automatically detected retry or bounce count, and $d_{\rm obj}$
the measured or manually audited object displacement. We define
\begin{align}
F_{\max} &= \max_t F_t,\\
\sigma_F^2 &= {\rm Var}_{t\in\mathcal{C}}(F_t),\\
\rho_{\rm drop} &= 1-\frac{|\mathcal{C}|}{T_{\rm expected}},\\
B_{\rm band} &=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}
\sum_{t\in\mathcal{C}}
\mathbb{I}\left[\tau_{\rm low}^{(k)} \le F_t \le \tau_{\rm high}^{(k)}\right].
\end{align}
For task $k$, the safe-success label is
\begin{align}
y_{\rm safe}
&= y_{\rm task}\,
\mathbb{I}[F_{\max}\le\tau_{\rm peak}^{(k)}]\,
\mathbb{I}[\sigma_F^2\le\tau_{\rm var}^{(k)}]\,
\mathbb{I}[\rho_{\rm drop}\le\tau_{\rm drop}^{(k)}]\nonumber\\
&\quad\cdot
\mathbb{I}[B_{\rm band}\ge\tau_{\rm band}^{(k)}]\,
\mathbb{I}[r\le\tau_{\rm retry}^{(k)}]\,
\mathbb{I}[d_{\rm obj}\le\tau_{\rm obj}^{(k)}]\,
\mathbb{I}[\neg{\rm damage}].
\end{align}
For insertion, $B_{\rm band}$ can be omitted or replaced by a jamming-duration
threshold. For wiping and swiping, the band-occupancy and variance terms are the
main indicators of stable contact. Any visible object displacement, tip-over,
grasped-object damage, emergency stop, or hardware safety intervention marks the
trial as unsafe; severe object movement or damage is also counted as task
failure even if the geometric endpoint is reached.

\subsection{Task Naming}

The rollout tables use five consistent task names. ``Board wiping'' denotes
planar blackboard erasing. ``Vase wiping'' denotes wiping a curved vase surface
while maintaining stable contact. ``Card swiping'' denotes sliding a card
through a constrained contact region, ``chip grasping'' denotes lifting a
fragile object without crushing or slipping it, and ``socket insertion''
denotes insertion under contact-force, jamming, retry, and recovery constraints.

\section{Network Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

\subsection{Module Overview}

\method{} is a modular add-on to a chunk-based action generator. The full
system contains four trainable or pretrained components:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a tactile encoder-decoder (TacVAE) that maps GelSight marker histories to
compact latent maps and decodes them back to marker fields;
\item a base action policy, usually Diffusion Policy, that provides the
behavioral prior over demonstrated action chunks;
\item an action-conditioned tactile foresight transformer that predicts the
future tactile latent sequence caused by a candidate action chunk; and
\item a contact-quality energy model that maps the predicted tactile future to
a scalar quality score and supplies gradients for action refinement.
\end{enumerate}

This separation is intentional. The base policy is not asked to learn a new
global objective at inference time; it only supplies candidate actions. The
foresight model answers a counterfactual contact question for each candidate
chunk. The scorer decides whether the predicted contact is desirable for the
task. The guidance module then applies a bounded local correction to the action
sample.

\subsection{TacVAE Tokens}

TacVAE produces a spatial latent rather than a single unstructured vector. In
the implementation, the latent has 16 channels and a $3\times3$ spatial
layout. The $3\times3$ cells are treated as tactile tokens after a linear
projection to the 512-D transformer hidden dimension. This keeps the foresight
module aware of where contact occurs on the GelSight surface. The decoder is
kept in the training and diagnostic loop so that latent prediction can be
checked in marker space.

\subsection{Action-Conditioned Foresight Transformer}

The foresight transformer receives the current observation tokens and the
candidate action chunk. Its prediction horizon is set to the same 16-step
window used by the policy chunk. The action sequence is embedded as the
conditioning signal for future contact dynamics. The current proprioceptive
state $q_t$ is included as context, but the future proprioceptive trajectory is
not used in the main model because it would leak an outcome rather than
condition on an executable candidate command.

\subsection{Contact-Quality Energy Model}

The scorer is trained to separate expert or acceptable contact from
task-specific failure modes. For board wiping, the failure modes include
pressure too small, pressure too large, and unstable pressure. For socket
insertion, the important negatives include side contact, bounce, retry-heavy
motion, jamming, and excessive peak force. The deployed scalar score is the
logit margin between the good-contact class and the log-sum-exp of bad-contact
classes. This score is less saturated than a probability and therefore provides
more useful gradients for late denoising.

\subsection{Guidance Module}

The guidance module runs only during selected late denoising or flow-matching
steps. It uses the current clean-action estimate, predicts the tactile future,
computes the quality margin, and differentiates the margin with respect to the
action sample. The update is normalized, clipped, and checked in raw action
units. In the main latent-only guidance path, the action features inside the
scorer are detached so that the gradient follows the intended path
\[
\action \rightarrow f_\psi(\obs,\action) \rightarrow \score.
\]
This reduces the risk that the scorer learns an action-only shortcut that does
not correspond to the predicted tactile consequence.

\subsection{TacVAE Pretraining Setup}

The tactile representation is trained before the policy and before the
foresight model. Each training sample is an eight-frame marker window from one
GelSight side. The target is the marker displacement field in the current
window, and the reconstruction loss is computed after decoding the latent back
to marker space. Marker fields are normalized per displacement channel using
statistics computed from the training split. The same normalization statistics
are saved with the checkpoint and reused by policy training, foresight

\subsection{Policy Training Setup}

The base DP policy is trained in a standard denoising-action framework. The
input condition is the concatenation of visual features, tactile latent
history, and normalized robot state over a short observation horizon. The action
target is a 16-step chunk in normalized \texttt{joint\_abs} action space. For
board wiping, the policy uses global and wrist RGB
views, \texttt{obs\_horizon}=2, \texttt{pred\_horizon}=16,
\texttt{n\_action\_steps}=8, batch size 64, 100 diffusion training steps, and
100 inference denoising steps. The model is trained for up to 1000 epochs with
validation-window monitoring and periodic best-checkpoint saving.

For tasks where the saved joint action labels are known to contain inverse
kinematics branch jumps, the policy should use end-effector action labels or
regenerated joint labels. This is especially relevant for older vase data,
where mixing a correct tactile model with an incorrect action label space would
make the foresight conditioning appear worse than it is.

\subsection{Scorer Training Setup}

The contact-quality scorer is trained from labeled contact windows rather than
from final task success alone. This gives dense supervision for contact quality:
expert contact, insufficient pressure, excessive pressure, oscillatory contact,
bounce, edge contact, jamming, and other task-specific negative modes. For the
board setting, the scorer has a held-out validation accuracy and
macro-F1 of 1.0 on the labeled validation windows used by the guidance
diagnostic. This result evaluates offline contact labels rather than online
task success.

At deployment, the scorer output is converted to an expert-margin energy. The
margin is preferred over a calibrated probability because it remains useful
when the classifier is confident. The same score can be used in two ways:
candidate reranking after sampling, or differentiable guidance during the final
denoising steps. The main \method{} configuration uses the latter because it
can locally improve a sampled chunk without discarding the base policy prior.

\subsection{Foresight Loss}

The foresight model predicts the latent sequence corresponding to the candidate
action chunk. Training uses frozen TacVAE targets and combines three terms:
\begin{align}
\loss_{\rm fs}
&= \loss_{\rm latent}
+ \lambda_m \loss_{\rm marker}
+ \lambda_\Delta \loss_\Delta,\\
\loss_{\rm latent}
&= {\rm SmoothL1}(\hat{\latent}_{1:H}, \latent_{1:H})
+ {\rm SmoothL1}(\hat{\latent}_H, \latent_H),\\
\loss_{\rm marker}
&= {\rm SmoothL1}(D_{\rm tac}(\hat{\latent}_{1:H}), \marker_{1:H}),\\
\loss_\Delta
&= {\rm SmoothL1}(\Delta\hat{\latent}_{1:H-1},
\Delta\latent_{1:H-1}).
\end{align}
The main configuration uses $\lambda_m=0.3$ and $\lambda_\Delta=0.5$. Decoded
marker loss keeps the latent forecast physically tied to marker displacement,
while the delta term penalizes temporally inconsistent predictions.

\subsection{Correct Action Conditioning}

The corrected training path sets \texttt{use\_state\_trajectory=false}.
For a sample starting at time $t$, the foresight input is
\texttt{actions/joint\_abs[t:t+H]} and the target is the tactile future
\texttt{marker\_offset[t+1:t+H]} or the stride-aligned equivalent. The previous
state-trajectory mode is kept only as a legacy option for ablations and is not
used for the main \method{} claim.

\section{Algorithmic Pipeline}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\noindent\textbf{Algorithm S1: Inference-time predicted-contact guidance.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Read the current observation $\obs_t=(I_t^g,I_t^w,q_t,
\marker_{t-W+1:t})$.
\item Encode the tactile history with TacVAE to obtain the current latent
$\latent_t$ and tactile tokens.
\item Run the base action generator to sample a denoising trajectory for an
action chunk $\action_{t:t+H-1}$.
\item At selected late denoising steps, convert the noisy action sample to the
clean action estimate $\hat{x}_0$.
\item Feed $\hat{x}_0$ into the action-conditioned foresight model.
\item Predict the future tactile latent sequence
$\hat{\latent}_{t:t+H_f-1}$.
\item Score the predicted tactile future with the contact-quality energy model:
\begin{equation}
s_{\rm margin}
= \ell_{\rm good}
- \log \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}_{\rm bad}} \exp(\ell_c).
\end{equation}
\item Backpropagate $s_{\rm margin}$ through
$\action \rightarrow \hat{\latent} \rightarrow \score$ and apply a normalized
small update inside a raw-action trust region.
\item Execute only the first action steps of the refined chunk and repeat at
the next control cycle.
\end{enumerate}

This design separates behavior generation from contact-quality correction. The
base policy remains the prior over demonstrated robot motions; foresight asks
what tactile consequence a candidate chunk would create; the scorer decides
whether that predicted future contact is desirable.

\section{Tactile Encoder and Reconstruction}
\label{sec:encoder}

\subsection{Encoder Construction}

TacVAE uses an encoder-decoder structure over marker displacement fields rather
than raw tactile images. The input is an eight-frame marker window; the encoder
maps it to a $16 \times 3 \times 3$ spatial latent and the decoder reconstructs
a $9 \times 9 \times 2$ marker field. The spatial latent keeps local contact
geometry available to foresight and the scorer, while reducing the regression
target from raw marker grids to a compact contact representation.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The reconstruction table shows that the marker field is low-dimensional enough
for simple baselines to reconstruct many static frames. This is why
reconstruction alone is not the full argument for TacVAE. The relevant property
for \method{} is whether the latent is useful in the full differentiable chain:
current tactile history $\rightarrow$ future tactile prediction
$\rightarrow$ contact-quality score $\rightarrow$ action update. TacVAE is
therefore evaluated together with foresight prediction and guidance
diagnostics, not only as a standalone autoencoder.

\section{Contact-Quality Scorer Validation}
\label{sec:scorer}

\subsection{Preference-Order Accuracy}

The scorer is validated with positive/negative rollout pairs constructed from
offline robot videos and their synchronized tactile logs. For each successful
rollout we select key contact frames around the decisive interaction; for each
failed rollout we select negative frames from the matched failure phase, such as
dropout, excessive pressure, bounce, side contact, or jamming. We then form
within-task positive/negative pairs and evaluate
\begin{equation}
A_{\rm pref}
= \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}
\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{P}}
\mathbb{I}\left[
S(\obs_i^+,\marker_i^+) >
S(\obs_j^-,\marker_j^-)
\right],
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the contact-quality margin score. This is the ranking property
needed by the energy model: the absolute score does not need perfect
calibration, but desirable contact should score above matched failure contact.

\section{Backbone Adaptation}
\label{sec:backbone}

\method{} is designed to wrap any chunk-based action generator. For DP, the
guidance gradient is applied directly to the denoising action sample. For RDT,
the same predicted-contact score is attached to the reactive diffusion
transformer action chunk. For $\pi_{0.5}$, the adaptation keeps the VLA
backbone in a flow-matching action-generation role and appends tactile latent
tokens to the policy prefix; the foresight branch remains the action-conditioned
contact consequence model. In all cases, the contact-quality scorer is outside
the base policy and can be evaluated independently.

The $\pi_{0.5}$ implementation uses 10 flow-matching integration steps and
activates ForeTac during the final two steps. At flow state $x_s$, the clean
action estimate is $\hat{x}_0=x_s-sv_\theta(x_s,s,\obs_t)$. The model action
space has 32 dimensions, while the robot command, tactile foresight condition,
and contact scorer use the first seven joint dimensions. We therefore mask the
score gradient to these seven executable dimensions. Padding dimensions follow
the unmodified base flow, and the guided robot command is constrained by the
same raw-action trust region used in the DP implementation.

\section{Failure Cases}
\label{sec:failures}

The most common failures are contact-quality failures rather than purely visual
pose failures.

\textbf{Board wiping.} Vision-only DP can cover the target path while applying
too little pressure for cleaning or too much pressure for safe wiping. The
typical trace alternates between contact dropout and high-force bursts. This
failure motivates scoring the predicted tactile future instead of only checking
the visual sweep trajectory.

\textbf{Vase wiping.} The curved and fragile object creates a narrow safe
force band. A visually correct path can still tip the vase if the normal force
oscillates or spikes. Older vase data also contains a label-quality issue in
some saved \texttt{joint\_abs} actions: the robot motion is smooth, but the
stored joint label can jump between inverse-kinematics branches. These episodes
should be used with end-effector labels or corrected joint labels.

\textbf{Card swiping.} The card task often fails through short contact dropout
or slot misalignment. The key tactile event is sustained sliding contact, not
only reaching the slot entrance. For this reason, short-horizon foresight is
less informative than a longer tactile sequence.

\textbf{Chip grasping.} The main expected failure modes are crushing and slip.
Successful behavior requires sufficient grip stability while keeping contact
forces below the damage threshold of the object.

\textbf{Socket insertion.} The insertion task fails through side contact,
bounce-lift-reinsert loops, and sustained jamming. Raw DP can generate an
apparently plausible approach but keep pushing after first contact. Tactile
concatenation improves reactivity but can still require several retries.
Foresight-guided policies reduce the peak force by anticipating the contact
consequence of the candidate chunk.

\section{Runtime and Deployment Details}
\label{sec:runtime}

\method{} adds two inference-time computations to the base action generator:
tactile foresight and contact-quality scoring. Both run on the candidate action
chunk and are batched over the chunk horizon. In the DP path, guidance is
activated only during late denoising steps, so the overhead is smaller than
running a planner over many full trajectories. The runtime update is normalized
and bounded in raw action units. If the score does not improve or the proposed
action update violates the trust region, the system can keep the base policy
chunk.

The deployment server records guidance diagnostics together with rollout
traces when enabled. These diagnostics include score before/after guidance,
gradient norm, accepted update norm, contact gate, and optional per-action
dimension gradient statistics. This logging is useful for separating three
cases: a weak scorer, a good scorer with no action gradient, and a valid
guidance path that still needs real-robot validation.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The method relies on the quality of the learned tactile foresight model and the
coverage of the contact-quality labels. When the candidate action leaves the
support of the demonstrations, the predicted tactile future can become less
reliable and the scorer may provide a weak correction. The trust-region update
limits this risk by keeping guidance local, but it also limits the size of the
possible improvement in a single control cycle. The system also assumes that
tactile marker fields are available and time-aligned with robot actions; large
tactile calibration drift or inaccurate action labels can reduce the benefit of
action-conditioned foresight.
