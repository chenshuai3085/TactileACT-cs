\begin{abstract}
Contact-rich manipulation requires a robot to choose actions not only by geometric
reachability, but also by the tactile consequences those actions will create.
High-precision insertion can fail from a small lateral contact or bounce before
the final insertion; surface wiping, card swiping, and delicate grasping require
contact that is neither too light nor too aggressive and remains stable over
time. We propose \fullmethod{} (\method), an inference-time framework that
guides a base visuomotor policy with predicted future tactile quality. A
Diffusion Policy first provides an action prior. A multi-step tactile foresight
model then predicts the future GelSight latent sequence induced by the current
clean-action estimate. Finally, a TacQuality energy scorer maps the predicted
future tactile sequence and action chunk to a differentiable contact-quality
score, whose gradient is injected into the late denoising steps. This design
keeps the learned policy as a strong behavioral prior while adding a proactive
contact signal before execution. We instantiate \method{} on five real robot
tasks: plug/socket insertion, blackboard wiping, vase wiping, card swiping, and
potato-chip grasping. The paper currently reports the full method, evaluation protocol,
and offline evidence that multi-step tactile consequences are separable and
provide finite action gradients; final paired real-rollout tables are left as
explicit placeholders to be filled after controlled baseline-versus-guided
experiments.
\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}

Robots often fail in contact-rich tasks for reasons that are nearly invisible
from images alone. In a high-precision plug/socket insertion task, two motions
can look similarly aligned while one produces a small lateral collision that
causes bounce, jamming, or repeated retries. In blackboard and vase wiping, the
visual trajectory may cover the intended surface while the contact is too light
to clean, too heavy for safety, or unstable across the stroke. In card swiping,
success depends on maintaining the correct sliding contact through a narrow
slot. In grasping a fragile potato chip, a small excess of tactile pressure can
break the object even if the gripper pose appears correct.

Modern imitation policies such as Action Chunking Transformers (ACT) and
Diffusion Policy learn expressive action distributions from demonstrations
\cite{zhao2023act,chi2023diffusion}. However, at inference time they usually
condition on the current observation and then execute a sampled action chunk.
Tactile input can make the current state estimate better, but a purely reactive
policy still answers the wrong question too late: it reacts after contact has
already happened. For delicate contact, the more useful question is prospective:
if the robot takes this candidate action chunk, what tactile consequence will it
create, and is that consequence desirable?

This paper updates the previous TacScore candidate-reranking formulation into
\fullmethod{} (\method), a base-policy-plus-foresight-plus-score-guidance route.
Instead of generating many full candidates and selecting one after sampling,
\method{} modifies the denoising trajectory itself. During the late diffusion
steps, it estimates the clean action $\hat{x}_0$, predicts the future tactile
latent sequence conditioned on that action, evaluates a learned tactile-quality
energy, and backpropagates the score through the foresight model to the action
sample. The update is intentionally small and normalized, so the base policy
remains the main action prior while tactile foresight supplies a proactive
contact correction.

The central claim is that future tactile consequences are useful not only as an
auxiliary representation, but as a differentiable action-quality signal. The
same framework supports two task families. For discrete precision tasks such as
plug/socket insertion, the tactile quality function penalizes predicted bounce,
edge contact, and jamming. For continuous-contact tasks such as wiping, card
swiping, and fragile potato-chip grasping, it rewards stable contact with task-appropriate
pressure and penalizes too-light, too-heavy, unstable, or rough tactile futures.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item We formulate proactive tactile consequence guidance for diffusion-based
contact-rich manipulation, moving from post-generation reranking to denoising-time
energy guidance.
\item We introduce a multi-step action-conditioned tactile foresight module that
predicts a future tactile latent sequence rather than a single endpoint, enabling
quality scores that depend on force stability and contact continuity.
\item We design a TacQuality energy scorer trained from semantic contact-outcome
labels, allowing the guidance signal to distinguish expert contact from
too-light, too-heavy, unstable, and task-specific failure modes.
\item We define a real-robot evaluation suite spanning insertion, blackboard
wiping, vase wiping, card swiping, and delicate potato-chip grasping, and provide the
offline checks required before safe online deployment.
\end{itemize}

\section{RELATED WORK}

\subsection{Visuomotor Imitation Policies}

Diffusion Policy models action chunks as conditional denoising trajectories and
has become a strong baseline for multimodal robot imitation
\cite{chi2023diffusion}. ACT reduces compounding errors by predicting action
chunks with a transformer policy \cite{zhao2023act}. Extensions improve
representations, inference speed, or task coverage \cite{ze2024dp3,prasad2024consistency}.
We use a diffusion policy as the base generator, but add an inference-time
objective that asks whether a proposed action will create good future contact.

\subsection{Tactile Manipulation and Prediction}

Tactile sensing is important for contact-rich manipulation because it reveals
contact location, shear, and pressure information that vision cannot reliably
observe \cite{calandra2018more,lee2019making,li2022see}. Recent work conditions
policies on tactile or force signals, including tactile-conditioned diffusion
and reactive slow-fast policies \cite{xue2025reactive,funk2025farm}. Other
methods learn visuo-tactile representations or world models that predict future
tactile states \cite{higuera2024sparsh,zhao2024t3,ye2025dreamtacvla}.
\method{} differs in how the prediction is used: the future tactile sequence is
not only an auxiliary feature, but the evidence for a differentiable action
quality score.

\subsection{Inference-Time Guidance and Verification}

Inference-time compute can improve robot policies by evaluating or steering
candidate actions. Implicit Behavioral Cloning scores actions with an energy
function \cite{florence2021ibc}; DynaGuide steers diffusion policies with visual
dynamics guidance \cite{du2025dynaguide}; verified-generation methods show the
benefit of sampling and checking multiple candidates \cite{saxena2025sitcom}.
Touch-based steering is closely related when tactile signals are used to guide
policy inference \cite{zhang2026touchguide}. Our focus is specifically on
action-conditioned \emph{future} tactile consequences. This is important because
current tactile alone may be neutral before contact, while the next action can
already imply bounce, overload, or contact dropout.

\section{PROBLEM FORMULATION}

At time $t$, the robot observes
\begin{equation}
o_t = \{I_t^{g}, I_t^{w}, q_t, m_{t-W+1:t}\},
\end{equation}
where $I_t^{g}$ and $I_t^{w}$ are global and wrist images, $q_t \in \Real^7$ is
the robot state, and $m_{t-W+1:t}$ is a recent GelSight marker-offset history.
The base policy predicts a future action chunk
$a_{t:t+H-1} \in \Real^{H \times d_a}$.

The objective is not only to imitate demonstrations, but to choose actions that
will create high-quality contact. We write the tactile foresight model as
\begin{equation}
\hat{z}_{t:t+H_f-1}=f_{\psi}(o_t,a_{t:t+H_f-1}),
\end{equation}
where $\hat{z}$ is a sequence of tactile latents. The TacQuality scorer returns
a scalar
\begin{equation}
S_{\phi}(a_{t:t+H_f-1},\hat{z}_{t:t+H_f-1},\tau),
\end{equation}
with task identifier or task semantics $\tau$. During inference, \method{}
approximately improves
\begin{equation}
\log p_{\theta}(a_{t:t+H-1}\mid o_t) +
\lambda S_{\phi}(a_{t:t+H_f-1}, f_{\psi}(o_t,a), \tau),
\end{equation}
where $p_{\theta}$ is the diffusion-policy action prior. The score is used only
as a local guidance signal; the policy prior remains responsible for keeping the
action in the demonstrated behavior manifold.

\section{METHOD}

\subsection{Base Diffusion Policy}

The base policy follows conditional denoising diffusion over action chunks. A
noise prediction network $\epsilon_\theta(x_i,i,o_t)$ predicts the noise at
diffusion step $i$. From the current noisy action sample $x_i$, the clean-action
estimate is
\begin{equation}
\hat{x}_0 =
\frac{x_i-\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_i}\epsilon_\theta(x_i,i,o_t)}
{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_i}}.
\label{eq:x0}
\end{equation}
The observation encoder uses image features, proprioception, and the current
tactile latent. In deployment, the model can be run either as a plain baseline
or with the guidance module enabled, using the same trained policy weights.

\subsection{Tactile Latent Representation}

The GelSight marker field is represented as a $9 \times 9 \times 2$ displacement
grid. We encode a short tactile history into a compact latent
\begin{equation}
z_t = E_{\rm tac}(m_{t-W+1:t}) \in \Real^{16 \times 3 \times 3},
\end{equation}
which is flattened to 144 dimensions for the foresight and scoring modules. The
tactile autoencoder is pretrained on task data and then frozen. Using the latent
space avoids raw marker regression as the central learning problem and provides a
structured state where contact magnitude, direction, and spatial distribution
remain available to downstream scoring.

\subsection{Multi-Step Tactile Foresight}

The foresight model predicts the tactile consequence of the candidate action:
\begin{equation}
\hat{z}_{t:t+H_f-1} =
f_{\psi}(m_{t-W+1:t}, q_t, a_{t:t+H_f-1}).
\end{equation}
Our current implementation uses $H_f=16$ and predicts all future latent steps,
not only the final latent. This matters for wiping, swiping, and delicate
grasping, where quality depends on whether contact is sustained and smooth
throughout the motion.

The training target is produced by encoding the real future marker windows with
the frozen tactile autoencoder. The loss combines latent reconstruction,
decoded-marker reconstruction, and temporal-difference consistency:
\begin{equation}
\loss_{\rm fs} =
\loss_{\rm latent} + \lambda_m \loss_{\rm marker}
+ \lambda_\Delta \loss_\Delta .
\end{equation}
The current blackboard model uses a 16-step action and tactile horizon, a hidden
dimension of 512, three transformer layers, and eight attention heads. For the
temporal-stride setting used in later experiments, both action and future tactile
indices are sampled at the same stride to keep training and deployment aligned.

\subsection{TacQuality Energy Scorer}

The scorer maps future tactile consequences to a scalar energy suitable for
guidance:
\begin{equation}
s = S_{\phi}(a_{t:t+H_f-1},\hat{z}_{t:t+H_f-1},\tau).
\end{equation}
Rather than hand-coding a single force formula, we train the scorer from
semantic contact-outcome labels. For blackboard wiping, the current label set is
\emph{expert}, \emph{pressure too small}, \emph{pressure too large}, and
\emph{pressure unstable}. For insertion, the negative modes include pre-bounce,
bounce, edge contact, and jamming. For vase wiping, card swiping, and
potato-chip grasping, the same design is reused with task-specific reason labels.

The current latent-energy scorer uses temporal MLP encoders for the action chunk
and the predicted tactile chunk, fuses the features, and compares the embedding
to learnable class prototypes. It is trained with cross entropy plus a margin
loss. The deployed scalar is an expert margin
\begin{equation}
s_{\rm margin} =
\ell_{\rm expert} -
\log \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}_{\rm bad}} \exp(\ell_c),
\label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
where $\ell_c$ are class logits. This score is preferred over raw $p({\rm good})$
because probability saturation can produce weak or unstable gradients.

\subsection{Denoising-Time Guidance}

At selected late denoising steps, \method{} applies the following update. First,
compute $\hat{x}_0$ using (\ref{eq:x0}) and transform it to the raw action space.
Second, align the action chunk with the foresight contract, for example
``shift1'' for older dense blackboard models or ``none'' for the temporal-stride-3
models. Third, predict $\hat{z}_{t:t+H_f-1}$ and compute the score
$s=S_{\phi}(a,\hat{z},\tau)$. Finally, backpropagate through the score:
\begin{equation}
g = \nabla_{x_i} s,\qquad
x_i \leftarrow x_i + \eta \frac{g}{\|g\|_2+\epsilon}.
\label{eq:guidance}
\end{equation}
We use a small guidance scale, a limited number of late guidance steps, gradient
normalization, and optional max-norm clipping. The blackboard latent-energy
deployment uses a latent-only path, meaning that the main gradient route is
action sample $\rightarrow$ foresight-predicted tactile latent $\rightarrow$
quality score, reducing the chance that the scorer exploits an action-only
shortcut.

\section{EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN}

\subsection{Tasks}

\textbf{Plug/socket insertion.} A plug must be inserted into a tight socket.
Failures include pre-insertion side contact, bounce, jamming, and repeated retry.
Metrics include success rate, insertion steps, retry or bounce count, peak force,
and side-force events.

\textbf{Blackboard wiping.} The robot wipes a flat board while maintaining
moderate and stable pressure. Metrics include coverage, force-band ratio, force
standard deviation, contact dropout ratio, and action smoothness.

\textbf{Vase wiping.} The robot wipes a curved fragile surface. This task
emphasizes safe upper-force bounds, stable contact under changing surface normal,
and avoiding object displacement.

\textbf{Card swiping.} The robot moves a card through a slot. Quality requires
continuous contact, correct card-slot alignment, and resistance that is neither
too small nor too large.

\textbf{Potato-chip grasping.} The robot grasps a fragile potato chip. The
tactile objective penalizes insufficient grip, slip, and excessive pressure that
can break the object.

\subsection{Baselines}

The planned comparisons are:
\begin{itemize}
\item ACT or task-specific behavioral cloning baseline.
\item Diffusion Policy without guidance.
\item Diffusion Policy with tactile conditioning but no foresight guidance.
\item Diffusion Policy with post-generation tactile reranking, when applicable.
\item Diffusion Policy with \method{} denoising-time tactile guidance.
\end{itemize}
For each online task, baseline and guided rollouts should be paired by reset
condition and object setup. The same policy checkpoint should be used whenever
possible so that the measured difference isolates the guidance module.

\subsection{Offline Checks Before Online Guidance}

Before running online guidance, we require three checks. First, foresight quality
is measured with latent SmoothL1/MSE, decoded marker error, and temporal-delta
error. Second, scorer generalization is evaluated with episode-level splits, not
frame-level random splits, using balanced accuracy, macro-F1, AUROC, and score
gaps. Third, guidance usability is tested by finite-gradient rate, positive
score-improvement rate after a small local action update, gradient norm, and
action-update norm. These checks catch the common failure mode where a classifier
is accurate offline but unusable as a guidance signal.

\subsection{Current Offline Evidence}

For the current blackboard latent-energy scorer, the episode-level validation
split separates the four contact-outcome classes in the collected distribution:
validation accuracy, macro-F1, expert-margin AUROC, finite-gradient rate, and
positive-gradient rate are all 1.0 in the logged run. A later temporal-stride-3
version preserves the same class separation and passes the deployment smoke test
for DP, foresight, scorer stride consistency, tensor shapes, and finite guidance
gradients. These results show that the score is available and differentiable.
They do \emph{not} by themselves prove real-robot task improvement; that claim
requires the paired rollout results specified below.

\section{ANALYSES AND ABLATIONS}

The following ablations are needed to support the method claims.

\textbf{Guidance versus no guidance.} Run the same base DP with
``disable-guidance'' and with \method{} enabled. This is the most important
comparison for the paper because it isolates inference-time tactile guidance.

\textbf{Reranking versus denoising guidance.} Compare best-of-$K$ tactile
reranking with the gradient-guided sampler. Reranking checks whether the scorer
can select better actions; denoising guidance checks whether the score can
locally improve a sampled action.

\textbf{Single-step versus multi-step foresight.} Replace the 16-step tactile
sequence with a single endpoint latent. We expect continuous-contact tasks to
benefit from the sequence model because pressure oscillation and contact dropout
are process-level failures.

\textbf{Score mode.} Compare $p({\rm good})$, expert margin, clipped energy, and
reason-aware energy. Expert margin should provide a more usable gradient when
classification probabilities saturate.

\textbf{Guidance path.} Compare action-only, tactile-only, and latent-only
guidance. The latent-only setting is the cleanest test of the causal route from
action to predicted tactile consequence to score.

\section{DISCUSSION}

\textbf{Why guidance rather than only reranking?} Reranking can select among
sampled chunks, but it cannot improve a single denoising path. \method{} uses
the same quality evidence to push the current sample during late denoising,
which is useful when the base policy already produces a near-valid action that
needs a small contact-aware correction.

\textbf{Why predict tactile instead of scoring current tactile?} Many failures
are determined before the bad contact is observed. Current tactile can be
uninformative while the candidate action already implies a future collision,
dropout, or overload. Foresight provides the differentiable bridge from action
to contact consequence.

\textbf{Why multi-step future tactile?} Contact quality is temporal. A single
final latent can miss a pressure spike, contact loss, or oscillation that occurs
mid-chunk. Multi-step foresight exposes these failure modes to the scorer.

\textbf{What claims are not yet justified?} The current offline results justify
that the blackboard contact classes are separable and differentiable in the
collected data distribution. They do not justify final success-rate claims until
paired online rollouts are completed for the target tasks.

\section{CONCLUSION}

We presented \fullmethod{}, a tactile-consequence guidance framework for
contact-rich robotic manipulation. The method combines a base diffusion policy,
multi-step tactile foresight, and a learned TacQuality energy scorer, then
injects the resulting score gradient into late denoising. This changes the paper
story from tactile candidate reranking to proactive tactile guidance: the robot
predicts what contact an action will create and adjusts the action before
execution. The framework naturally covers high-precision insertion, blackboard
and vase wiping, card swiping, and fragile potato-chip grasping. The remaining
experimental priority is to fill the placeholder tables with paired
baseline-versus-guided real-rollout metrics.
